The Sunday Turnaround Cleaning Checklist

What needs to happen between Sunday morning services and the next major use.

Sunday morning is the highest-traffic time in most faith facilities. Between 9am and noon, a sanctuary may see three services, a fellowship hall two coffee hours, a nursery 40 children, and a kitchen enough coffee urns and serving trays to require a full commercial washdown. The question for the facilities coordinator is: what needs to happen before the building is usable again on Wednesday?

Immediately After the Last Service

The first hour after the last service is the window for tasks that require the building to be warm, the lights on, and access unrestricted. This includes collecting bulletins and disposable items left in pews, returning hymnals and Bibles to correct positions, emptying nursery diaper pails, and collecting kitchen equipment for washing. Volunteer teams handle most of this well.

The Sanctuary

For the sanctuary itself, the Sunday turnaround checklist covers: removing any program materials or flowers left behind, vacuuming pew rows and carpet aisles, spot-cleaning any obvious marks on pew surfaces, emptying offering plate holders, and checking the altar area for items that need to be returned to the sacristy. This work can be done by a crew in two to three hours for a mid-sized sanctuary, depending on how well post-service tidying was done.

What the Sunday turnaround does not need to include: full pew wipe-down, window cleaning, hard floor scrubbing. Those belong to the weekly professional clean scheduled for Saturday or Sunday evening.

The Fellowship Hall and Kitchen

Coffee hours generate more cleaning work than any other regular church activity per square foot. Spilled coffee on carpet or VCT flooring needs attention within the hour; dried coffee stains are significantly harder to remove. Kitchen surfaces — especially coffee urn stations — should be wiped down before the end of Sunday programming.

A kitchen that handles coffee service for 200 people needs: counter wipe-down, sink and faucet cleaning, urn and decanter washing or rinsing, floor sweep of the kitchen area, and refrigerator check for uncovered food. This is 30–45 minutes of work with two people and appropriate supplies.

The Nursery

Nursery turnaround after Sunday services is the most time-sensitive part of the checklist for two reasons: health and licensing. Hard contact surfaces — changing tables, toy surfaces, door handles, light switches — need to be disinfected before the next use. If your facility operates a licensed childcare program on weekday mornings, the turnaround window may be as short as Sunday afternoon to Monday 7am.

A quick Sunday nursery checklist: diaper pail emptied and relined, changing table surface disinfected, toy surfaces wiped, floor vacuumed, trash removed, hand sanitizer dispensers checked and refilled.

What a Professional Clean Adds

Volunteer Sunday turnaround handles the immediate visible work. Professional weekly cleaning handles what accumulates beneath it: carpet soil that vacuuming alone does not extract, hard floor buildup on grout lines and corners, kitchen grease at range height, and restroom scale. If your facility relies on volunteers for all cleaning, the surfaces look clean but accumulate more quickly than facilities that supplement with professional service.
